# Granular and Cohesive Soil Flow/Failure Benchmarks for Validating a Real-Time MPM Soil Simulator

Target simulator: Newton implicit MPM, 8 cm grid (dx = 0.08 m), 4 cm particles (about 2 particles per cell per axis), Drucker-Prager with cohesion.

Note on resolution: the simulator has dx = 0.08 m. A benchmark is only resolvable if the failing mass spans at least about 10 to 20 cells, so roughly 1 to 2 m or more. Most lab benchmarks (0.1 to 0.5 m) cannot be run at physical scale. The workable approach is dimensionless comparison: run the benchmark geometry scaled up (for example 10x to 50x), match friction angle, and scale cohesion to keep the cohesion number c/(rho g H) constant. That works only if the benchmark obeys known scaling laws (see below). These are inferences, not sourced facts.

## Granular column collapse (dry, cohesive/wet, large-scale), runout scaling laws, data availability

### Takeaway
Granular column collapse is the most widely used MPM/SPH/DEM validation case. Experiments report a piecewise relation between runout and aspect ratio a = H0/L0: linear below a transition near a ~ 1.7 to 3, and a power law above it with an exponent of about 2/3 (2D) or about 1/2 (axisymmetric). Because it is dimensionless and largely independent of grain size, it is the best first benchmark for an 8 cm grid when scaled up to metre size. The aluminium-rod 2D test (Bui et al. 2008 / Nguyen et al. 2017) is the standard quantitative MPM case, with digitised free surfaces at fixed times. Cohesive column collapse has a growing literature organised around a Bond or cohesion number.

### Cited Findings
**Dry column scaling laws**
- Lube et al. and Lajeunesse et al. ran axisymmetric collapse of granular columns onto a flat horizontal plane and gave runout scaling laws as functions of initial aspect ratio. Lube et al. also ran quasi-2D experiments between two vertical walls, and Balmforth & Kerswell (2005) studied how the wall gap affects quasi-2D experiments. — [Warnett et al. 2014 (RG)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259633896_Scalings_of_axisymmetric_granular_column_collapse); [Staron/Hinch-type DEM study arXiv physics/0501022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0501022)
- The relation is linear in aspect ratio for short columns and a power law for tall columns. A study of gravity level effects (Computers and Geotechnics 2025) reports a critical aspect ratio of a = 1.7 for the change in the piecewise power law, unchanged by the gravity level. That supports dimensionless comparison and centrifuge analogues. — [Granular column collapse: effects of gravity levels, Comput. Geotech. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0266352X25001569)
- Numerical studies report power-law exponents of 0.68 to 0.77 for a > 4, consistent with experiments. — [Influence of Cross-section Shape on Granular Column Collapses, arXiv 2206.00915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00915) (snippet-level, could not open the full PDF because of its size)
- Warnett, Denissenko, Thomas, Kiraci & Williams (2014), "Scalings of axisymmetric granular column collapse," Granular Matter 16(1):115-124, DOI 10.1007/s10035-013-0469-x. Later work summarises its finding that increasing the system size r* increased the mobility of dry collapses, a size effect that breaks pure aspect-ratio scaling. — [Inertial effects in just-saturated axisymmetric column collapses, Granular Matter 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10035-023-01326-x); [Man et al. 2021 Finite-size analysis, GRL](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021GL096054)
- Man et al. (2021, GRL) "Finite-Size Analysis of the Collapse of Dry Granular Columns" shows that runout depends on column size relative to grain size, not only on aspect ratio. — [GRL 2021](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021GL096054)
- Further dry-collapse references for time histories and deposit morphology: "Collapse dynamics of dry granular columns: from free-fall to quasi-static flow" [arXiv 2112.11458](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.11458); "Deposition morphology of granular column collapses" [arXiv 2002.02146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02146); polydispersity effects on dry and immersed collapse [JFM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/polydispersity-effect-on-dry-and-immersed-granular-collapses-an-experimental-study/C46602E37CD5BC6F30D88233AC875777).

**Aluminium-rod 2D collapse (the standard MPM quantitative test)**
- Setup: a rectangular column of aluminium rods 0.2 m wide by 0.1 m high (a = 0.5), released by fast removal of a wall and filmed with a high-speed camera. The deposit stops at about 0.607 s, with final runout about 0.48 m from the wall. The rods barely move out of plane, so the test is genuinely plane strain. — [Xie, Navas & López-Querol, implicit B-spline MPM, arXiv 2609.26104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26104)
- Calibrated parameters as used in MPM: unit weight 20.4 kN/m3, E = 5840 kPa, nu = 0.3, friction angle 21.9 deg, c = 0. Rods are 1 mm and 1.5 mm in diameter and 50 mm long. — [arXiv 2609.26104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26104); [regolith sampling SPH, arXiv 1705.00893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.00893)
- Measured outputs: runout versus time, and free-surface profiles at t = 0.109, 0.235 and 0.607 s, digitised from the experiment. The implicit B-spline MPM used 2.5 mm cells (about 40 cells across the column height), 115,200 material points and dt = 2.5e-4 s, with no numerical damping. — [arXiv 2609.26104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26104)
- Attribution: sources credit the test both to Bui et al. (2008, SPH, elastic-perfectly plastic, cohesive and non-cohesive collapse) and to Nguyen et al. (2017, Landslides 14(1):69-81, SPH with viscous damping and stress regularisation). Bui et al. 2008 appears to be the origin and Nguyen 2017 a re-use or re-documentation, but this is unconfirmed. — [Monash: Nguyen et al. 2017](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-new-sph-based-approach-to-simulation-of-granular-flows-using-vi/); [SPH SIMSAND collapse, ASCE IJG](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/(ASCE)GM.1943-5622.0001255)

**Cohesive and wet column collapse**
- Langlois et al. (2015, JGR Earth Surface), "Collapse of a two-dimensional brittle granular column": a DEM study that defines a granular Bond number Bo = C/(m0 g). Larger Bo reduces runout. For Bo >> 1, Bo has little further effect on fragmentation. — [Langlois et al. 2015](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2014JF003330)
- Abramian, Staron & Lagrée: "Collapse of a cohesive granular column" and "How cohesion controls the roughness of a granular deposit" (Soft Matter 2021). These are grain-resolving and continuum studies in which cohesion shortens the deposit and roughens its surface. — [Lagrée PDF](http://www.ida.upmc.fr/~lagree/TEXTES/PDF/collapse-of-a-cohesive-granular-column.pdf); [Abramian et al. SM 2021](http://www.lmm.jussieu.fr/~staron/Publi/Abramian-SM-21.pdf)
- A continuum model adding a constant cohesive yield stress to mu(I) ("The Slumping of a Cohesive Granular Column: Continuum and Discrete Modelling") finds the cohesion parameter mainly controls final runout, with little effect on collapse velocity. That is relevant to Drucker-Prager with cohesion, because cohesion should change the deposit far more than the flow kinematics. — [RG 344807155](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344807155_Cohesive_slump_The_Slumping_of_a_Cohesive_Granular_Column_Continuum_and_Discrete_Modelling)
- Artoni et al. and Santomaso et al. are cited for wet (pendular) column collapse experiments, framed by a Bond number and water content. They give a phase diagram with continuous collapse, block collapse and no collapse. — [arXiv 2402.07285, effects of interparticle cohesion on collapse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07285) (summary via search, full PDF too large to fetch)
- Search summaries disagree on direction: one says runout rises with Bond number, while Langlois says larger Bo shortens runout. The likely cause is that Bo is defined in opposite senses (cohesion/gravity versus gravity/cohesion). Check each paper's definition before comparing. — [search summary of Abramian / Langlois](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2014JF003330)

**Large-scale flows**
- USGS debris-flow flume (H.J. Andrews Experimental Forest, Oregon; built 1991, used since 1992). Iverson et al. (2010, JGR) aggregates 28 large-scale experiments from 1994 to 2004, with debris properties, flow-front velocities, deposit geometries and surface waves. — [Iverson et al. 2010](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2009JF001514)
- Open USGS data: a ScienceBase data release with sensor data (3 CSV files) from experiments in May 2017 (Iverson & Obryk 2019), and video from 1992 to 2017 (USGS OFR 2007-1315 v1.4, 2018). USGS data are generally US public domain. — [ScienceBase](https://sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/5b44ee03e4b060350a1408f1); [Eos](https://eos.org/science-updates/a-new-era-of-debris-flow-experiments-in-the-oregon-woods)
- These flows are water-saturated and strongly pore-pressure driven, so they are a poor match for a dry Drucker-Prager model. — [Iverson 2020 memoir](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2019CN000117)

**Open datasets (column collapse)**
- Zenodo, all CC-BY 4.0: saturated sand column collapse simulation ([10.5281/zenodo.4043064](https://zenodo.org/records/4043064)); landslide fluidization column collapse ([zenodo 4449579](https://zenodo.org/records/4449579)); buoyant granular column collapses, 40.8 MB ([zenodo 15036788](https://zenodo.org/records/15036788)); dry column collapse with multi-scale grain shape, surface points and a shape-index code ([zenodo 13899286](https://zenodo.org/records/13899286)); CFD-DEM submarine collapse ([zenodo 13439264](https://zenodo.org/records/13439264)).
- GNS surrogate for granular flows (Kumar & Vantassel, arXiv 2305.05218): trains on MPM column collapses that follow the Lajeunesse and Lube experiments. Its search snippet points to no public trajectory dataset. — [arXiv 2305.05218](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05218)

### Inferences
- Primary dry benchmark: a 2D plane-strain column at a = 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8, scaled so L0 is about 2 to 4 m (25 to 50 cells). Compare normalised runout (L_inf - L0)/L0 and final height H_inf/L0 against the experimental piecewise law. Grain-size and finite-size effects (Warnett, Man) mean the absolute coefficient carries roughly 10 to 20% uncertainty, so treat the exponent and the transition ratio as the main pass/fail criteria.
- The aluminium-rod test can be run at 25x scale (5 m by 2.5 m, about 62 by 31 cells). This assumes the Mohr-Coulomb material is rate-independent, so time scales as sqrt(L) and runout scales linearly. Compare runout(t * sqrt(g/H0)) and the three digitised free-surface profiles. The reference MPM used about 40 cells per column height, so a 31-cell version is a fair resolution test.
- For cohesion: sweep the cohesion number c/(rho g H0) in a scaled column test and check (a) a no-collapse threshold consistent with the vertical-cut critical height Hc ~ 4c/gamma * tan(45 + phi/2) (classical Rankine, not sourced here), and (b) block versus continuous failure regimes, compared qualitatively against the Artoni/Santomaso phase diagram.
- Drucker-Prager is rate-independent, unlike mu(I). It will match final deposits but not necessarily front-velocity histories for tall columns (a > 3), where inertial effects matter.

### Gaps
- Exact published coefficients were not verified against the primary papers. From memory (unverified): Lube 2004 axisymmetric (r_inf - r0)/r0 ~ 1.24a for a < 1.7 and ~ 1.6a^(1/2) for a > 1.7. Lube 2005 2D ~ 1.2a for a < 2.3 and ~ 1.9a^(2/3) for a > 2.3. Lajeunesse 2004 axisymmetric transition near a ~ 3. Balmforth & Kerswell exponent ~ 0.65 for a narrow channel and ~ 0.9 for a wide one. Confirm these against JFM 508 (2004), JFM 541 (2005), Phys. Fluids 17 (2005), and JFM 538 (2005) before use.
- Not found: open raw profile data from Lube or Lajeunesse. Their published plots must be digitised.
- No large-scale (metre-plus) dry column collapse with open data was found. The Lube/Cole pyroclastic large-scale experiments (PELE facility, NZ) were not located in this search.
- Not found: open experimental tabulations from Artoni/Santomaso wet-collapse work.

## Angle of repose / heap formation and silo/hopper discharge (Beverloo)

### Takeaway
Beverloo's law, Q = C rho_b g^(1/2) (D - kd)^(5/2), is the standard flow-rate check. It is independent of fill height. It is a good integration test of a continuum model's flowing and arrest states, but at dx = 0.08 m the orifice must be about 0.5 m or wider (6 cells or more), and the grain-size correction kd cannot be resolved. Angle of repose is the simplest static check: a Drucker-Prager heap should settle near the input friction angle (inference). No dedicated MPM angle-of-repose benchmark source was found.

### Cited Findings
- Beverloo form Q = C rho g^(1/2)(D - kd)^(5/2), where D is the orifice size and d the grain diameter. Fill height plays no role. — [Purdue Wassgren dimensional analysis notes](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~wassgren/teaching/ME30900/Examples/dim_anal_18.pdf); [Rev. Mex. Fís. 65 (2019)](https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/rmf/v65n2/0035-001X-rmf-65-02-139.pdf)
- DEM validation of Beverloo through a hopper (arXiv 2512.03698) reports deviation at large D/d. Cone angle, outlet edge and wall friction change the fitted constants. — [arXiv 2512.03698](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03698)
- Dunatunga & Kamrin (2015, JFM 779:483-513, arXiv 1411.5447): an MPM framework for granular flow that handles dense flow and stress-free separation. It is validated against 2D column collapse and 3D impact experiments, and simulates silo drainage, inclined-plane flow and slug impact. — [arXiv 1411.5447](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.5447); [JFM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/continuum-modelling-and-simulation-of-granular-flows-through-their-many-phases/5FC7EDA190762F08DF6A4526946796FC)
- Silo clogging with nonlocal granular fluidity, JFM 2022 (Kamrin group): predicts flow spreading and clogging at small openings, which a local Drucker-Prager model cannot capture. — [arXiv 2107.03624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03624); there is also a 2026 comparison of non-local models for silo discharge, [arXiv 2608.26460](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26460)

### Inferences
- A 2D or 3D hopper test at dx = 0.08 m with D of 0.4 to 1.6 m: check that Q is constant in time (no fill-height dependence) and that Q ~ D^(3/2) in 2D or D^(5/2) in 3D. Fit C against the published range for sand, typically about 0.55 to 0.65 in 3D (from memory, unverified). Local Drucker-Prager will not clog, so skip small-orifice cases.
- Heap test: pour from a point source or collapse a column at a = 0.5 and measure the final slope angle against the input phi. Expect a slight underestimate from numerical dissipation and shear-band smearing at coarse resolution.

### Gaps
- No MPM-specific angle-of-repose or Beverloo validation paper with tabulated data was found in this search.
- The C and k constants for specific sands were not sourced here.

## Cohesive soil failure: vertical cuts, trenches, retaining walls, footings, slopes, landslides (Selborne, Vajont, Aberfan)

### Takeaway
For cohesive and frictional soil, the most usable benchmarks for a coarse real-time MPM are: (1) analytical or limit-analysis references at field scale (Griffiths & Lane slope factor of safety, Rankine/Terzaghi earth pressure and bearing capacity, critical vertical-cut height), which are scale-free and resolution-friendly; (2) Fang & Ishibashi (1986) wall-movement earth pressure tests as a classic 1g dataset; and (3) field-scale MPM landslide cases (Selborne cutting, Vajont-type compound slide) for qualitative runout. Field-scale cases suit an 8 cm grid best but have little open data.

### Cited Findings
- **Griffiths & Lane slope** (standard MPM/FEM cohesive benchmark): H = 10 m, slope 26.57 deg (2:1), gamma = 20 kN/m3, E = 100 MPa, nu = 0.3, phi = 20 deg, c = 10 kPa. Factor of safety by strength reduction is about 1.35 to 1.4 (FEM fails to converge at SRF = 1.4). A B-spline MPM used 1 m cells and 21,540 material points, and its failure surface reaches the slope toe at SRF = 3.0 post-failure. — [arXiv 2609.26104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26104)
- **Strip footing with large penetration** on near-incompressible Tresca soil (Monforte et al. PFEM reference): E = 100 kPa, nu = 0.495, c = 1 kPa, phi = 0. The comparison is normalised pressure against penetration, which should approach Prandtl's (2 + pi)c. — [arXiv 2609.26104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26104)
- **Fang & Ishibashi (1986)**, "Static earth pressures with various wall movements," J. Geotech. Eng. ASCE 112(3):317-333. A rigid wall in dry sand with a free horizontal surface, tested in three modes: rotation about top, rotation about heel, and translation. A companion paper covers the passive case. Data at a rotation of 1.5 mrad are commonly taken as the active state. A centrifuge study of dry sand under active wall movement correlated with Fang's distributions at 0.83 to 0.99 (mean 0.92). Khosravi et al. (2013) is another common 1g dataset. — [Scispace record](https://scispace.com/papers/static-earth-pressures-with-various-wall-movements-43clkim062); [Cambridge repository: earth pressures in dry sand with active rigid wall movement](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/b4c7ab68-7286-4d07-be1f-5347afd0052c/content); [Passive earth pressures with various wall movements (PDF)](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~frosch/ftp/Talbott/11%20-%20References/files/Passive%20Earth%20Pressures%20with%20Various%20Wall%20Movements.pdf); [ASCE JGGE rotating model wall](https://doi.org/10.1061/JGGEFK.GTENG-12163)
- **Vertical cuts in unsaturated "cohesionless" soil**: Strathclyde work extends shear strength with matric suction and plasticity bounds. It includes a simple experiment, column collapse tests on fine silica sand, and a field case of large safe cut heights in pozzolan. This is relevant because "apparent cohesion" in construction-site sand comes from suction. — [Strathclyde ENGEO paper](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/48098/1/ENGEO_3563.pdf); [pure portal](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/publications/an-approach-for-predicting-the-stability-of-vertical-cuts-in-cohe/)
- **Selborne cutting** (MPM, Yerro/Alonso/Pinyol, Soga co-author on some): a 9 m cut slope in Gault Clay brought to failure by raising pore pressure. Parameters come from field data and the recharge follows piezometer readings. MPM reproduced progressive failure and a final runout close to the field observation. — [Modelling Progressive Failure with MPM (RG)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265136576_Modelling_Progressive_Failure_with_MPM); [Alonso, Yerro, Pinyol 2015, UPC](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstream/2117/81776/1/2015_Alonso_Yerro_Pinyol%20-%20Recent%20developments%20of%20the%20MPM%20for%20the%20simulation%20of%20landslides.pdf)
- **Vajont-type compound slide**: strain-softening Mohr-Coulomb in MPM, covering the whole sequence from triggering to runout, with failure progressing along an internal shear band as pore pressure rises. — [Internal Progressive Failure in Deep-Seated Landslides, RMRE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00603-015-0888-6)
- Soga et al. 2016 Géotechnique Lecture / Cooling Prize paper "The material point method in slope stability analysis." Bandara & Soga (2015) model progressive levee failure from infiltration using MPM. — [GE Plus](https://www.geplus.co.uk/technical-paper/cooling-prize-paper-the-material-point-method-in-slope-stability-analysis-25-05-2017/); [RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320563967_The_Material_Point_Method_in_Slope_Stability_Analysis)
- A sequential hybrid FEM-to-MPM method for slope failures (Kumar group, arXiv 2407.05185). — [arXiv 2407.05185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05185)

### Inferences
- The best fit for an 8 cm real-time engine is the scale-free analytical set: Griffiths & Lane slope at reduced size (H = 2 to 5 m, c scaled to keep c/(gamma H) = 0.05), the vertical-cut critical height, Prandtl/Terzaghi bearing capacity for a strip footing about 1 m wide (12 cells), and Rankine active/passive thrust on a 2 to 3 m wall. Each gives a single pass/fail number.
- Fang & Ishibashi's wall was about 1 m high (from memory, unverified). At dx = 0.08 m that is only about 12 cells, which is marginal but usable for total thrust, not for the pressure distribution.
- Selborne (9 m cut) and Vajont run at 8 cm resolution only as long as ~10^6 to 10^7 particles fit. They need pore pressure or softening, which a pure Drucker-Prager-with-cohesion model can only approximate by scheduled strength reduction.

### Gaps
- No open-data trench or vertical-cut collapse experiment for cohesive soil with time histories was found. Centrifuge trench and cut tests (for example from Cambridge or UC Davis) were not located.
- No open MPM benchmark repositories were found for Aberfan. Bui et al.'s SPH levee and slope validation and Bui et al.'s 2008 cohesive soil collapse parameters were not retrieved.
- Fang & Ishibashi's raw data are not openly licensed. They must be digitised from the ASCE paper, which is paywalled.

## Benchmarks used by MPM/continuum codes (Anura3D, CB-Geo, Uintah, Taichi, Newton/Warp, Chrono CRM, Kamrin RFT)

### Takeaway
Across codes, the shared validation set is: column collapse (dry, sometimes cohesive or saturated), slope stability or failure, footing penetration, and, for terramechanics codes, wheel, cone or sphere intrusion. Chrono::CRM (mu(I) continuum SPH) validates against sphere cratering, cone penetration, single-wheel tests (MGRU3) and reduced-gravity traction. Kamrin's group validates against column collapse, impact and wheel experiments, and compares MPM with granular RFT. CB-Geo keeps public benchmark problems on GitHub.

### Cited Findings
- Anura3D (2017 slides): lists column collapse among its applications, shows collapsing column displacements (v2017.1), and is used for multiphase wet-sand column collapse. — [DSD-NL 2017 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/dsdnl-2017-anura3d-mpm-for-geotechnical-engineering-achtergrond-rohe/77132175); [Multi-scale multiphase granular flows](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309606618_Multi-Scale_Multiphase_Modelling_Of_Granular_Flows)
- CB-Geo MPM keeps benchmark problems in a public GitHub repository (cb-geo/mpm-benchmarks, MIT-licensed code per the CB-Geo project; licence not confirmed in this search). — [CB-Geo MPM slides](https://kks32-slides.github.io/2019-mpm/)
- Chrono::CRM ("A Physics-Based Continuum Model for Versatile, Scalable, and Fast Terramechanics Simulation," arXiv 2507.05643): mu(I) rheology, with soil-solid interaction posed as fluid-structure interaction. Validation includes three studies against physical tests and one against DEM: sphere cratering against an empirical penetration law, cone penetration, and the MGRU3 single wheel against DEM. Chrono documents claim accuracy similar to DEM at much lower cost. — [arXiv 2507.05643](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05643v1); [Chrono terrain docs](https://api.projectchrono.org/vehicle_terrain.html)
- Chrono's CRM has been used to optimise rover wheels, with designs checked on a single-wheel testbed. — [NASA NTRS 20230017332](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230017332); [arXiv 2602.01535](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01535v1)
- Kamrin group: Dunatunga & Kamrin 2015 MPM, validated on 2D column collapse and 3D impact experiments at chute inclinations of 45 to 65 deg. — [arXiv 1411.5447](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.5447). Projectile impact MPM (JMPS 2017) — [RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311656729_Continuum_modeling_of_projectile_impact_with_dry_granular_media).
- Agarwal et al. (2019, J. Terramechanics): compares granular RFT with continuum plasticity MPM for wheels in dry granular media, and finds the plasticity approach accurate. Agarwal, Karsai, Goldman & Kamrin (2021, Science Advances) "Surprising simplicity in the modeling of dynamic granular intrusion" adds Dynamic RFT, tested on wheel locomotion experiments in poppy seeds. Li et al. (2013) introduced 2D RFT. — [Terramechanics 2019 PDF](https://crablab.gatech.edu/pages/publications/pdf/Terramechanics-2019.pdf); [arXiv 2005.10976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10976.pdf); [Agarwal MIT thesis 2022](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/144625/1/agarwal-ssnk_agl-phd-meche-2022-thesis.pdf)
- Implicit B-spline MPM (Xie et al., arXiv 2609.26104) validates against the aluminium-rod collapse, the Griffiths & Lane slope, and large-penetration strip footing. Only the modified F-bar variant matched the experimental runout history. Locking mitigation matters for implicit MPM, which is directly relevant to Newton's implicit MPM. — [arXiv 2609.26104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26104)
- A dataset on benchmarking and verifying discrete granular simulations (Data in Brief 2024). — [ScienceDirect S235234092400221X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235234092400221X)

### Inferences
- A suite matching peer codes: (1) dry column collapse at several a, (2) aluminium-rod collapse, scaled; (3) Griffiths & Lane slope factor of safety; (4) strip-footing bearing capacity; (5) Rankine wall thrust; (6) hopper flow rate; (7) Chrono-style cone penetration and sphere cratering for excavator and bucket interaction. Items 1 to 5 are dimensionless and transfer to an 8 cm grid by scaling.
- Implicit MPM with roughly 2 particles per cell per axis risks volumetric locking under near-incompressible plastic flow. The Xie et al. result, where only the F-bar variant matched runout, suggests making F-bar or B-bar style anti-locking a validation axis.

### Gaps
- Not found in this search: Taichi MLS-MPM, Uintah, Newton/Warp MPM and Klar et al. sand validation specifics. Klar et al. 2016 (SIGGRAPH Drucker-Prager sand) is known from memory to compare visually, not quantitatively. A "Hu et al." Chrono CRM paper could not be matched to an author.
- Licence for the CB-Geo benchmark repository was not verified.
- Not found: open wheel or intrusion raw data from the Kamrin and Goldman experiments. Check the Science Advances supplementary material.
