Feasibility review · 2 Nov 2026 · updated with browser decision and physics research

Construction site simulation on NVIDIA SimReady + Isaac Sim

Can we build a construction site simulation with full, realistic physics on NVIDIA's free SimReady assets, the way someone recently showed a warehouse?

Scope: the three source links, NVIDIA's SimReady asset library (scanned in full), Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab physics, and our DGX Spark workstation.

§1Verdict

Yes, with two large gaps. NVIDIA's stack can run a physics-based construction site: rigid bodies, jointed machines, collisions, sensors. The free assets give us about 40% of what a site needs: barriers, scaffolds, beams, pallets, tools, forklifts. They contain no heavy machines and no soil or terrain. Those two parts are where the real work is.

We don't have to use Omniverse. The assets are plain OpenUSD files and load outside NVIDIA's tools. The recommended setup is a hybrid: the DGX Spark runs the realistic physics, and a three.js page in the browser shows it. Omniverse / Isaac Sim stays optional.

Ready

Site layout & props

Fences, scaffolds, ladders, beams, pallets, tools, forklifts. All have mass, colliders and friction set.

Build or source

Heavy machines

Excavators, cranes, trucks and loaders are missing. Import from CAD or URDF and rig the joints ourselves.

Experimental

Digging & soil

Newton's granular solver handles small patches. Excavator-to-soil coupling is unproven in Isaac.

§2What the source links show

  • X post (@SahilExec, 31 Oct 2026). A 25-second repost of a video by @anxuanng (codepawl). It claims Claude Opus 5.5 built a warehouse with racks, forklifts and picking/packing zones from SimReady assets. There's no code or repo. The same group published codepawl/simready-glb, the assets converted to GLB, which drops physics data. Our inference: the demo was most likely a 3D layout with scripted motion, not a physics simulation. We could not watch the video.
  • Hugging Face: SimReady Central. A free library of 2,000+ OpenUSD assets with mass, friction, colliders, joints and semantic labels. It covers robotics, warehouses, factories, AI-factory racks and household items. Construction is not mentioned.
  • NVIDIA blog: agents building simulations. Frontier AI agents assemble scenes with Isaac Sim, Newton physics, Warp and the new ov* libraries. All the examples are warehouses, kitchens and factories. Terrain and soil are not covered.

§4Browser or Omniverse

The assets work in a three.js browser stack. We installed the standard USD Python library (usd-core 26.11) on our DGX Spark. It read the masses, joints, colliders and friction values from a real asset (the forklift) without any NVIDIA software. A third party has converted 2,595 of the assets to web-ready GLB files (codepawl/simready-glb, 228 GB down to 3.2 GB), with the physics data added back in. In their tests, the converted physics matched the originals.

Loading USD directly in the browser isn't practical yet. three.js r187's USD loader can't fetch multi-file assets over the web and ignores physics. The best full in-browser USD library is licensed for non-commercial use only. So the route is: convert the assets offline, then load GLB files plus a physics data file.

OptionRealismWhat it means
Hybrid (recommended)HighestThe DGX Spark runs soil, machines, hydraulics and cables (NVIDIA Newton, with Project Chrono as the fallback). It streams compact positions and terrain changes to a three.js page. Many viewers, no video encoding, and the GPU is left free for physics.
All-browserReducedJolt physics in the browser for the machines, plus a small neural network that predicts how the ground changes, trained on the server's simulations. Good for demos and training; not accurate for digging forces.
Omniverse / Isaac Sim streamingHigh, limitedPhotoreal video streamed to the browser. Isaac Sim's web viewer allows one viewer per instance with no login. Use it for checking scenes, camera and LiDAR simulation, and a few high-quality views.

Why the browser can't do the digging alone: browser GPU particle soil reaches about 100k–300k particles in real time. That's only a few cubic metres, those demos are fluids rather than sand, and none produces accurate digging forces. Chrome on ARM Linux, like our DGX Spark, also needs a flag to enable WebGPU.

§5How realistic the physics can be

BehaviourFidelityApproach and limits
Machines driving, lifting, carrying; collisions; falling objectsGoodPhysX rigid bodies and articulations. Hydraulic cylinders become motor-driven joints with force limits.
Crane cables and swinging loadsApproximateA chain of linked rigid segments. It works, but needs tuning for stability and stretch.
Digging sand, soil, gravel; stockpilesLimitedIsaac Lab 3.0 has Newton's implicit MPM solver for granular material. Two-way coupling with rigid bodies is in an experimental module. We found no published excavator example. Realistic for a few cubic metres, not a whole site in real time.
Cameras, LiDAR, synthetic training dataGoodRTX sensor simulation with semantic labels from the SimReady assets.

If earthmoving has to be truly realistic: the industry tool is AGX Dynamics. Its terrain module has working excavator and loader demos, but it's commercial and runs in Unity or Unreal, not Omniverse. Project Chrono is a free alternative with a soil model.

§6What published research says about soil

We surveyed soil and terrain simulation research worldwide. Each method trades realism against speed; only one has published accuracy at real-time speed.

MethodBest validated accuracySpeedOpen implementation
Height map + particles near the bucket10–25% vs DEM; ~10% vs fieldReal time, >1000× faster than DEMNone (AGX is commercial); method is published
GPU MPM (particles on a grid)Sparse; real-excavator task transfer in 2026Near-interactive in a small zoneNewton implicit MPM (experimental)
GPU SPH (Chrono CRM)3 physical tests + digging benchmark"Near interactive" at scaleChrono CRM (BSD-3)
Terramechanics (Bekker-Wong, SCM)Good for wheels and tracksReal time on CPUChrono SCM
GPU DEM~10% vs field bucket filling100–10,000× slower than real timeChrono DEM-Engine
FEM / CEL8.8% vs soil binHours per caseAbaqus (commercial)
  • Best real-time method: keep the ground as a height map and turn only the soil near the bucket into particles (Servin and colleagues, Umeå / Algoryx, 2021). It's within 10–25% of slow, high-detail particle simulation (DEM) while running more than 1,000× faster.
  • Calibration beats detail: field tests on a real wheel loader showed a ~10% gap between simulation and reality, and more simulation detail barely improved it.
  • Real excavator: in September 2026, ETH Zurich trained a controller in GPU particle soil and ran it on an 11.5 t excavator. It built a 42 m embankment at an expert operator's pace. This is a preprint, and it shows the task works, not that the forces are accurate.
  • Machines: realistic feel comes from hydraulics (pump, valves, cylinder pressures) computed inside each physics step. Most robotics simulators skip this and command joint speeds directly.
  • Cranes: heavy loads on light ropes break game-style physics. Use a rope model built for heavy loads (adaptive-resolution wire or direct-solve rods), with the hook and load as full rigid bodies so sway and twist emerge naturally.
  • Gaps nobody has filled: no validated digging forces in wet clay or mud, no shared excavator benchmark, and no public dataset pairing digging forces with lab-measured soil. That calibration will be our own work.

Isaac Sim officially supports the DGX Spark; it's the only ARM machine NVIDIA supports. Known issues: some installs need a libgomp library preload to start, and users report rendering and PhysX GPU crashes on this chip. Check the driver against the current requirements page before installing. Our driver (580.178) is on the R580 branch NVIDIA requires for Isaac Sim on this machine; avoid upgrading to R590, which crashes Isaac Sim's renderer.

The GB10's memory bandwidth (about 205 GB/s measured) is roughly 1/6–1/8 of an RTX 5090's, so particle soil runs slower per step. The 128 GB of memory lets much larger offline simulations fit. Rough budget: about 1 million soil particles, real time only inside small zones around the tools (an estimate to measure in Phase 0). NVIDIA's Warp and Newton officially support this machine. The commercial soil engines (AGX, Vortex) don't document ARM support, so they would probably need a separate x86 machine.

§8Recommended architecture and build plan

Assets
Offline on the Spark: read physics data from the USD files, start from the converted GLBs, and model the missing machines (excavator, crawler crane, dump truck, dozer) in USD so one source feeds both server and browser.
Server physics
NVIDIA Newton/Warp on the GB10: site height map, particle soil zones around each tool, machines with in-step hydraulics, heavy-load crane ropes, animated workers. Project Chrono is the fallback.
Transport
Compressed positions (about 0.5 Mbit/s for 100 machines and props at 30 Hz), terrain tile changes, and only the soil particles that are moving.
Browser
three.js WebGPU page with the GLB assets, a deformable terrain, soil particles, and Gaussian-splat captures of a real site for photoreal surroundings.

  1. Platform smoke tests

    Run Newton's sand example, the Isaac Sim 6.2 container, a Chrono build with GPU modules, and Chrome WebGPU on the Spark. Exit: measured particle throughput and a go/no-go per engine.

  2. Assets and static site

    Physics-data extractor, check the 2,595 converted assets against the full set, three.js page with physics props, and an attribution page. Exit: a site of SimReady props loads in the browser with correct mass, joints and colliders.

  3. Soil core

    Height map plus particle zone around the bucket, tested against Chrono CRM on the same bucket pass. Exit: real-time single bucket pass on the GB10, and the engine choice is made.

  4. Machines

    Excavator, dozer, dump truck and crawler crane with hydraulics, tracks on soft ground, and crane hoist ropes. Exit: a stable dig cycle and a full-load lift without rope stretch.

  5. Calibration and validation

    High-detail DEM reference runs, public bucket-force data, real excavator logs. Exit: bucket force and fill within about 10–25% of DEM, with the field error documented.

  6. Streaming and multi-user

    Live state stream, real-site backdrops, an optional Omniverse high-quality view. Exit: a remote browser shows a live dig at 30 FPS over the local network.

  7. Browser-only tier

    Train the ground-prediction network on Phase 4 data and run it in the browser with Jolt machines. Exit: an all-browser demo with its error measured against the server.

§9Open questions for the team

  • Is realistic digging a must-have, or is digging limited to a few zones acceptable?
  • Which machines matter most, and do we have manufacturer CAD files or a budget for 3D models?
  • What is the simulation for: visual planning, operator training, robot or AI training, or safety analysis? The answer changes the fidelity we need.
  • Can we get real machine data (bucket forces, hydraulic pressures) from a partner or an instrumented rig? Public data won't be enough to validate realism.
  • Would we add an x86 machine and a commercial license (AGX Dynamics) if Newton's soil coupling falls short?
